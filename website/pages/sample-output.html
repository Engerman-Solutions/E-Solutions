Executive summary

May revenue of $1,318,000 exceeded budget by $68,000 (+5.4%), driven by new ARR bookings of $214,000 against a $180,000 plan (+19%) on two mid-market expansion deals, partially offset by approximately $9,000 of timing slippage on a delayed annual renewal that has since closed in early June. Gross profit of $962,000 was $24,000 (+2.6%) above plan, but gross margin compressed to 73.0% against 75.0% planned as cloud-hosting and data-egress costs scaled with new-logo onboarding faster than the current committed-use discount tier captured. Operating income of $95,000 came in $23,000 (19.5%) below the $118,000 plan, as sales & marketing ran $46,000 over to fund the new account-executive ramp and an incremental demand-generation test. Cash closed at $4.2M with 16 months of runway against a >12-month floor.

Recommendation: The growth story is intact and bookings are accelerating; the revenue beat is real. Two items require Board attention this period — unit-cost discipline on hosting, which is a repeating exposure rather than a one-time miss, and sales efficiency, where CAC payback has drifted to 14 months against a 12-month target. We recommend authorising a move to a higher committed-use discount tier before next close, and holding incremental demand-generation spend until payback trends back under 12 months.

Variance drivers

Revenue (+$68,000 favorable, +5.4%)

  • New ARR bookings of $214,000 beat the $180,000 plan (+19%), led by two mid-market expansion deals that closed in the final week of the month.
  • Partially offset by approximately $9,000 of timing slippage on a delayed annual renewal. That contract has since closed in early June and will land in the following period.

Gross margin (73.0% actual vs 75.0% plan, unfavorable)

  • Cloud-hosting and data-egress costs scaled with new-logo onboarding faster than the current committed-use discount tier captured, driving cost of revenue $44,000 over plan.
  • This is the single largest margin item this month, and it is a repeating exposure — not a one-time miss. It will recur every month at the current commitment tier until the contract is renegotiated.

Sales & marketing (($46,000) unfavorable, (12.3%))

  • Driven by onboarding the new account executive — ramp and tooling — plus an unbudgeted demand-generation test.
  • Pipeline created supports the spend, but CAC payback extended to approximately 14 months against a 12-month target. Worth watching before committing further incremental spend.

R&D / product (+$12,000 favorable, +4.3% — timing)

  • A contractor hire slipped from May into June. The underspend is timing-related, not a reduction in roadmap investment; expect normalisation next month.

General & administrative (($13,000) unfavorable, (7.9%))

  • Elevated by one-time legal and audit-readiness fees. Expected to return to run-rate in June; flagged for confirmation in the Q3 reforecast so the items are not carried forward in error.

Cash & forecast commentary

Cash position closed at $4.2M. Months of runway at current burn: 16 months, against a >12-month floor — comfortably on track. Net revenue retention held at 108% versus a 105% target, and gross revenue quality is unchanged.

Forward signal: bookings momentum is genuine and the pipeline supports the sales investment. The constraint on this business is not demand — it is unit economics. Both watch items this month sit on the cost side of the equation, and both are addressable within the quarter.

Risks & watch items

  • Gross-margin compression. Hosting costs are scaling ahead of committed-use discounts, running 2.0 points below plan. Without action this persists and compounds as new logos onboard.
  • CAC payback drift. The sales & marketing overspend is investable only if bookings efficiency holds. Payback should be monitored monthly against the 12-month target.
  • G&A normalisation. Confirm the legal and audit-readiness fees are genuinely one-time so the Q3 reforecast does not carry them forward as run-rate.
